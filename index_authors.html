TL;DR

CondVLN is a scene-graph-grounded benchmark for conditional instruction following in vision-language navigation. Its instructions name the goal only through conditions on the scene, so an agent must check each condition, take the branch the scene supports, and only then navigate. Every condition's truth is known by construction, so the benchmark separates verified successes from lucky ones. Our neurosymbolic reference agent does this; seven VLN agents reach the goal in a few percent of episodes, mostly without checking.

Abstract

Vision-language navigation agents are typically evaluated on their ability to follow route-like instructions toward a fixed goal. Real navigation instructions, however, often depend on the observed state of the environment: if a condition holds, then follow one path, otherwise take another. Existing evaluations provide limited control over conditional branch execution, making it difficult to determine whether agents fail because of perception, grounding, navigation, or logical decision-making. We introduce CondVLN, a scene-graph-grounded benchmark and controlled testbed for conditional instruction following in vision-language navigation. CondVLN programmatically generates instructions whose branch conditions are grounded in verifiable 3D scene-graph predicates, with controlled variation in branch depth, dependency chain length, spatial composition, evidence observability, and instruction horizon. Our dataset holds 19,952 generated instructions across 34K objects and 1.88M spatial relationships in AI2-THOR, Matterport3D, Gibson, and ReplicaCAD, with 1,998 verified evaluation episodes, scored by standard VLN metrics and two branch diagnostics, Branch Selection Accuracy and Conditional Success Rate. Seven VLN agents (NaVid, NaVILA, CA-Nav, Open-Nav, VLN-Zero, StreamVLN, SNav-7B) reach a named object in a few percent of episodes, and most of their successes on branching episodes bypass the required branch, a failure that success rate and path length do not show. A neurosymbolic agent that checks each condition explicitly follows the correct branch on significantly more episodes, showing what condition following buys. Ablations on identical episodes show that the remaining potential performance gain is in acquiring the necessary evidence, not in choosing the correct branch.

Benchmark

Instructions are generated from 3D scene graphs: every condition is a scene-graph predicate whose truth is known by construction, conditions decide the destination, and they are composed through controlled nesting depth and chain length. Agents receive camera observations and the instruction text only. Branch Selection Accuracy (BSA) credits the condition objects an agent reaches in the order the instruction checks them; Conditional Success Rate (CSR) requires the goal and the full branch.

Instructions follow six templates that vary nesting depth (1 to 3) and chain length (1 to 3 branches): simple, nested, deep nested, chain, long chain, nested chain. The verified evaluation sets are balanced across the six categories, and about two thirds of the episodes carry subgoals (condition objects the branch must visit).

Neurosymbolic reference agent

The neurosymbolic agent turns one instruction into a program and runs it in three stages:

1Parse. GPT-4.1 rewrites the instruction as an if/then/else program over scene-graph predicates.

2Search and verify. One condition at a time: sweep in place, detect, lift detections into a 3D memory, explore frontiers under a budget. An exhausted budget declares the condition false.

3Execute. Walk to the target the program selects and stop within the success radius.

Three oracles (resolved branch, condition truths in words, perfect perception) remove one source of difficulty each, so the ablations attribute the remaining cost to evidence acquisition rather than to branch logic.
